---
title: "Starting with the problem, not the solution"
date: 2024-11-01
description: "Why LooMed begins with patient control, not hospital buy-in"
---

Most attempts at medical record interoperability start by trying to convince hospitals to cooperate. This is backwards.

The fundamental problem isn't technical interoperability — it's control. Patients don't control their medical records. Hospitals do. And hospitals have no incentive to change that.

So LooMed starts with a different question: **What if patients could build their own medical records, independent of hospital systems?**

## The bootstrap problem

If you start by assuming patients can request and receive their records (which is legally required in most jurisdictions), then you can build a patient-controlled record system without needing hospital cooperation first.

Steps:
1. Patient requests records from hospital (legal right)
2. Hospital provides records (usually PDF or paper)
3. Patient digitizes and stores in LooMed format
4. Over time, patient accumulates complete history

This is slow. It's manual. But it's **possible today** without changing hospital systems.

## Growing from the edges

Once patients have their own records:
- They can share them selectively (new doctor, second opinion)
- They can aggregate across multiple hospitals
- They can add their own data (wearables, symptoms, notes)
- They control access and revocation

Eventually, if enough patients use this, hospitals have an incentive to make it easier. But the system doesn't depend on that happening.

## What this means for design

The protocol must:
- Work with messy, inconsistent input formats
- Allow gradual improvement of data quality
- Support manual entry as a first-class citizen
- Be useful even with partial records

This is not elegant. But it's pragmatic. The perfect protocol that requires hospital cooperation will never exist. The imperfect one that patients can use today might actually get built.
