title: "Emergency access and the impossible tradeoff"
date: 2024-12-15
description: "The tension between security and accessibility in medical records"
---

If a patient is unconscious in an emergency room, how does the doctor access their medical records?

This is the classic objection to patient-controlled medical records. And it's legitimate.

## The false choice

The usual framing is: either you have strong security (patient-controlled access) **or** you have emergency access. Pick one.

But this is a false choice. The current system doesn't actually solve this problem well either.

In today's system:
- Emergency rooms often can't access records from other hospitals
- Paper records get lost
- Systems are incompatible
- Critical information is missing

So we've already accepted that emergency care happens without complete medical histories. Doctors work with what they have.

## What patient control adds

With LooMed, a patient could:
- Designate emergency access delegates
- Store emergency info in easily accessible format
- Carry a physical backup (QR code, USB key)
- Grant time-limited access to emergency contacts

This isn't perfect. But it's not worse than the current system. And it's better in non-emergency cases.

## The real question

The real question isn't "How do we solve emergency access perfectly?" It's "Is patient-controlled access valuable enough in the 99% case to accept imperfect emergency access?"

I think yes. Because the current system is already imperfect in emergencies, and terrible in non-emergencies.

## Design implications

For LooMed, this means:
- Emergency summary as a first-class feature
- Multiple access methods (delegate keys, recovery codes, physical backup)
- Clear UX around emergency scenarios
- Honest about limitations

Don't promise perfect emergency access. Promise better control for everyday medical care, with reasonable emergency fallbacks.

---

This will be controversial. But pretending there's a perfect solution is dishonest.
